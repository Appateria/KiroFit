# Panel — consumer power users (n=6)

Participants labelled P1–P6. All had ≥14 consecutive logging days at recruitment;
by interview date **3 of 6 had lapsed** (no log in ≥7 days).

## Task observation: "show me how you decide what to train this week"

| Participant | Where the week's plan lives | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Notes app, rewritten weekly | transcribes each session into Kiro Fit after doing it |
| P2 | Printed PDF from a coach | app used purely as a receipt |
| P3 | Nowhere — repeats last session | 11 consecutive identical sessions in the log |
| P4 | Spreadsheet with a 4-week block | maintains totals in the sheet, not the app |
| P5 | Memory | "whatever I did last time, maybe a bit longer" |
| P6 | Spreadsheet + coach messages | copies monthly totals out by hand |

Not one participant used Kiro Fit to decide anything. **Four of six maintain a parallel
artefact** — a notes file or a spreadsheet — specifically to hold the plan or the
monthly rollup the app does not provide.

## Task observation: "tell me your current weight as the app has it"

The two US participants (P1, P4) both had implausible values stored. P4's profile read
`154 kg`. She had entered her weight in pounds four months earlier and never noticed,
because nothing in the product ever shows weight back in context.

## Verbatims

> P3: "It never tells me what to do next. So I do the same thing. I've done the same
> 30-minute run eleven times and the app is perfectly happy about it."

> P1: "I write the week in Notes on Sunday, then I type it into the app after each
> session. I'm the integration."

> P4: "It asked me for kilograms. I typed my weight. I'm American — I typed pounds.
> That was in March and it's still wrong, and honestly it doesn't matter because the
> number never appears anywhere."

> P6: "I want the month. One screen: this month versus last month, what changed. I
> rebuild it in a spreadsheet on the first of every month, which is absurd."

> P2: "My coach asks for a summary before each session. I screenshot seven separate
> weekly views and stitch them together in Photos."

> P5: "I lapsed because there was nothing that noticed I lapsed. No streak, no nudge,
> no email. It's a very polite app."

## Lapse post-mortems (the 3 who stopped)

| Participant | Days logged | Stop trigger | Would return if |
|---|---|---|---|
| P3 | 17 | boredom — no progression, no variety | app writes the week |
| P4 | 21 | forgot for two days, never resumed | reminders + streak |
| P5 | 15 | "no sense of getting anywhere" | monthly progress view |

## Ranked ask (forced choice, 100 points to allocate, n=6, totals)

| Ask | Points |
|---|---|
| App generates a training plan | 171 |
| Weekly / monthly progress report | 148 |
| Pick my own units (lb, ft/in, miles) | 88 |
| Correct or remove a logged session | 71 |
| Streaks + reminders | 44 |
| Offline logging | 18 |

## PM takeaways

1. **Prescription outranks recording.** Every top ask is about being told something —
   what to do next, or what has changed. The logging path itself drew zero complaints;
   it works, and nobody values it on its own.
2. **The parallel spreadsheet is the signal.** Four of six users built an external tool
   to do the two jobs at the top of this list. That is unmet demand with a measured
   workaround cost, not a preference.
3. **Units are cheap and load-bearing.** Two of six had corrupt body metrics from a
   two-day fix. It is not a preference item; it silently poisons any future trend or
   report built on those values.
4. **Plan and report are one problem, not two.** P1, P4 and P6 all described the same
   loop: decide the week, do the week, review the week. Shipping either half alone
   leaves the loop broken.
5. Offline ranks low for this segment — do not let the loudest support ticket theme
   outrank the panel's actual priority order.
